Verify, Don't Rewrite

A critical review of the first plan, with every claim fact-checked against the sources, followed by a narrower thesis. The new version answers one research question and measures one kind of guarantee honestly. It is built on data the vision-language model hasn't seen in training.

Part 1

What was wrong with v1

Two fact-checks were run: one on data, contamination and tooling, the other on overlapping recent work and the statistics (with a bound simulation). Findings are ordered by how much damage they would do.

CRITICAL

The planned VLM has already seen most of the test sets

CHURRO's published training sources (Appendix E) include READ 2016, Bentham (ICDAR 2015 release), the Nationaal Archief VOC/notarial ground truth, and most of CATMuS Medieval's parts. Three of the four planned evaluation collections are in its training data.

Fix: Split collections into seen and clean tiers (see Data). Seen collections can still test whether the guarantee holds, since calibration stays valid even on data the model has seen. They can't support claims about how much text gets accepted, because that number is inflated. Headline results come from a clean collection.

CRITICAL

Too many contributions, none finished

v1 was a method paper (the gate), a statistics paper (the guarantee), a benchmark paper (HalluTrap), a systems paper (cost model, review UI) and a search study, all at once. At 8 months with 1–2 people, each would be half-done.

Fix: One research question (Part 2). The benchmark slices become the evaluation tool, not a separate contribution. The search study, the cost-per-page headline and the review user study are cut.

CRITICAL

The guarantee isn't the contribution; the score behind it is

Conformal calibration can wrap any confidence score. v1 treated certification as the novelty and plain baselines as a table row. Yet simple signals may be very strong. Humphries (May 2026) flagged words where two LLMs disagree and caught 76% of errors while reviewing only 4% of the text.

Fix: The thesis becomes a controlled comparison of acceptance signals under one valid certification procedure. The measure is coverage at a certified α. If cheap signals win, that is still a useful and publishable answer.

MAJOR

The statistics were invalid as specified

(a) The sample-size table treated words as independent, but words in a line are correlated. Gurram (arXiv 2608.14639, Jul 2026) shows the naive field-level procedure breaks under exactly this clustering, with a design effect of 1.8–2.5. (b) The error rate among accepted words is a ratio, so plain Learn-then-Test on it is not valid. (c) "One line from each random page" still lets several lines from the same page into the sample. (d) At 400 lines, α = 1–2% is not realistically certifiable.

Fix: The line is the unit, sampled uniformly from the whole collection. The ratio is linearised into a line-level mean. The target is α ≈ 3% at δ = 0.1, and the sample-size curve is reported as a result.

MAJOR

The planned recogniser is stale, and the verifier needed training data a real archive lacks

PyLaia's last release is 1.1.2 (Oct 2024), and it pins torch 1.13 and Python below 3.11. It has no N-best output and no forced alignment. v1 also fine-tuned the verifier on each collection, which takes thousands of labelled lines a new archive doesn't have.

Fix: Kraken + McCATMuS (CC BY 4.0; 118k lines, 16th century to today, 7 languages), used off the shelf as the realistic setting. Fine-tuning becomes an upper-bound condition. Use pyctcdecode for N-best lists, and torchaudio.functional.forced_align, whose planned deprecation was reversed.

MAJOR

The error annotation plan assumed you can read German Kurrent

Transcribing never-online pages and hand-classifying 300 errors both need someone who can read the script. There is also little early modern English handwriting ground truth with a clean licence: Folger EMMO has no line coordinates, and Hooker and Sloane are already in CHURRO.

Fix: The clean collection is 18th–19th-century English that you can read, from an archive whose pages have never been transcribed online. Budget and measure transcription time in month 1.

MAJOR

Page-level VLM vs line-level pipeline

CHURRO was trained on whole pages of up to 5,120 patches, so line crops are out of distribution. Aligning page-level output back to lines and words is real engineering work and introduces its own errors. v1 hid this.

Fix: A month-1 pilot decides between CHURRO (page level, then aligned to lines) and Qwen3-VL-8B at 4-bit (~12 GB, line crops). Whichever wins, the line–word alignment is an explicit, tested component.

MAJOR

Ground truth made by post-editing would carry the VLM's own errors

The quick way to build ground truth is to correct a VLM's draft. That biases the ground truth toward the VLM's plausible readings, the very hallucinations the thesis measures.

Fix: Test and calibration lines are keyed blind, with no model output shown, and 10% are double-keyed to measure agreement. Only the gate-tuning split may use post-editing.

MINOR

Novelty has narrowed since the first proposal

New close work:

  • Gurram 2608.14639 (selective risk control for LLM document extraction)
  • FaithC4 2607.21617 (VLMs "rewrite" printed pages)
  • Grounded in Sound 2609.14455 (a frozen CTC model judges ASR output, but as a training reward)
  • Isom 2507.04132 (HTR + LLM correction; flags a line when two runs disagree)

None combines a separately trained visual verifier with a line-level certificate on historical handwriting.

Fix: Claim that combination and nothing broader. Check the ICDAR 2026 table of contents by hand, and repeat the search at M1 and M5.

MINOR

Cost and review-time headlines rest on assumptions

The review time was simulated, so cost per useful page at 10⁶ pages was a model, not a measurement.

Fix: Log GPU-seconds and API spend per page as engineering telemetry. Drop the cost claim from the headline.

Part 2 · Re-proposal

One question

Research question

At a certified error bound, which acceptance signal lets the most historical handwriting through without review? And does checking against an independent visual recogniser beat model disagreement and plain confidence?

Sub-questions

  1. Signal: How does coverage at α = 3% compare across VLM token probability, disagreement between two VLMs, CTC confidence, CTC–VLM agreement, the visual support ratio, and a learned combination?
  2. Where it fails: Which tokens do the signals miss? Look especially at names, numbers, archaic spellings, abbreviations, and text invented under occlusion.
  3. Validity: Does the line-level guarantee hold over repeated resplits? How badly does it break when calibration comes from a different collection or hand?
  4. Cost of the guarantee: How do coverage and bound tightness change with 300, 500 and 1,000 calibration lines?

Why every outcome is useful: if the visual verifier wins, that is a method result. If cheap disagreement signals do just as well, archives learn they don't need the extra model. Either way the certification protocol and the harness remain.

Part 3

System design

The title is now meant literally: the system never rewrites. It keeps the VLM's word, or it flags that word and shows the recogniser's reading and the image crop beside it. That removes v1's edit-extraction stage, and with it a whole class of errors the corrector itself could introduce.

One optional extension (only if G3 passes)

Arbitration: when a word is flagged, replace it with the CTC reading if S5 prefers it, then measure introduced vs fixed errors. This is the "rewrite" v1 relied on. It is tested here as a separate, secondary question, with the same harness and splits.

Part 5

The guarantee, stated correctly

Unit: a line, drawn uniformly at random from the whole collection. Pages are never used as the sampling unit. Test and calibration lines come from disjoint pages.

Headline guarantee (PAC, via Learn-then-Test): for each line i, let Wi be accepted-and-wrong words, Ai accepted words and mi total words. "Error among accepted ≤ α" is equivalent to E[(Wi − αAi)/mi] ≤ 0. That is a mean of bounded, independent line-level values. Rescale to [0,1] and test each threshold with a Hoeffding–Bentkus or Waudby-Smith–Ramdas p-value. Thresholds are tested in a fixed order, strictest first, set on the gate-tuning split, and testing stops at the first failure.

Also reported: joint risk (accepted-and-wrong ÷ all words, which is monotone, so fixed-sequence testing is clean), a lower bound on coverage, and the weaker expected-risk CRC version for comparison.

What 400 lines can realistically certify

From the simulation: δ = 0.1, 100 runs per cell, realistic line model (1 + Poisson(8) words; 15% hard lines at 10× error). The table shows the median upper bound on joint risk.

For the ratio claim at n = 400 with 80% coverage: if the signal really achieves ≤1% error among accepted words, α = 3% is certified essentially every time and α = 2% only about 5% of the time. Certifying α = 1% needs roughly 1,500+ lines. Plain Hoeffding bounds are useless at these error levels (5–8%).

Target claim: "On [collection] (never published online), calibrated with 400 uniformly sampled lines, signal S6 auto-accepted 7x% of words; with 90% confidence the error rate among accepted words is ≤ 3%. The strongest baseline (S2) reached 6x% at the same bound. When calibrated on Bentham instead, the bound was violated in V% of resplits."

Part 7

Engineering deliverables

These should be assessable as software engineering in their own right, not as scaffolding.

Architecture

Ports-and-adapters pipeline: proposer, verifier, signal and certifier are interfaces with swappable implementations, configured from one file. Architecture decision records for the major choices (CHURRO vs Qwen, Kraken vs PyLaia).

Correctness

A split generator with property-based tests that assert page-disjointness and uniform line sampling. The LTT module is tested against simulated data with known risk (the violation rate must stay ≤ δ). Alignment is tested on synthetic insertions and deletions.

Reproducibility

A record/replay cache for every model call (model ID, version, date, prompt hash). Container images. Seeds and configs committed. A one-command rerun of every table, verified on a fresh machine in M8.

Interoperability

PAGE-XML output with per-word state (accepted/flagged), alternatives and provenance, importable into eScriptorium and Transkribus. A minimal viewer shows flagged words next to their image crops.

If two people: one owns the proposer, verifier, signals and statistics. The other owns the harness, split generator, keying workflow, cache, PAGE-XML and viewer. The contract between them is a line-level JSONL schema fixed in M1.

Part 8

Draft abstract

Vision-language models now transcribe historical handwriting cheaply and often accurately. Their errors, though, are fluent: plausible words, modernised spellings and "corrected" names that are not on the page. Archives cannot proofread millions of pages, so the practical question is not how accurate a model is on average, but which of its words can be released without review, and how sure we can be. We compare six signals for deciding which words to accept, from the model's own token probabilities and disagreement between models to a visual support ratio computed by forced-aligning the VLM's reading under an independent CTC recogniser. Every signal goes through the same line-level Learn-then-Test procedure, which certifies an upper bound on the error rate among accepted words. On a never-published collection of 18th–19th-century English manuscripts, and on two public collections the model has already seen, we report how much text each signal releases at a certified 3% bound. We also report which kinds of error escape each signal, how often the guarantee is violated under repeated resampling and across collections, and how much calibration data it needs. We release the harness, the certification code and the clean-collection annotations.

Prerequisites to secure in the next two weeks

  • The clean collection. An archive willing to let you use 150–200 never-transcribed pages of 18th–19th-century English for research, and ideally to release the annotations. Ask a university special-collections department or a county record office.
  • Supervisor agreement that a comparative study with a possibly negative result counts as a thesis contribution.
  • A GPU with ~12 GB (university machine or Colab/Kaggle) for Qwen3-VL-8B at 4-bit or CHURRO 3B, plus a small, capped API budget if you want one commercial comparison.
  • Washington dataset licence.
